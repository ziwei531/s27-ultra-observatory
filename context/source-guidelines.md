# Source and evidence rules

1. Independently search and fetch sources. Search snippets are discovery only. Never infer a leak from a headline alone.
2. Write short original summaries, not copied articles. Preserve publication/updated dates separately from our first observation date.
3. Distinguish the publisher from the underlying tipster. Repeated reporting of the same tip is not independent corroboration.
4. Low = single rumor chain or speculation; medium = inspectable attributed reporting with specific evidence, still unconfirmed; high = independently corroborated primary evidence. Confidence is an editorial judgment, not probability.
5. Status: reported, disputed, superseded, confirmed. Contradictory claims cite both sides and link related claims. Superseded requires explicit newer evidence and a replacement identifier, not merely a newer date.
6. Only official Samsung evidence can establish an announced Samsung specification. Do not imply that none exists merely because a search failed.
7. Baseline is the real first collection date. Each material update creates a new immutable JSON snapshot. No-change checks create a check record, not a new snapshot.
8. Exclude inaccessible and low-quality material from claims unless another readable report supplies the evidence; disclose the provenance gap. Never bypass captchas.
9. Use original clearly labeled illustrative artwork only unless redistribution rights are documented. Original illustrations are not leaked renders or predictions of exact industrial design. Store attribution in JSON.
10. Keep quotes minimal and never commit full publisher pages, contact details, tokens, or third-party tracking assets.
